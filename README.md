# Shapes of Constant Width

*An interactive math learning prototype by Kamila Woronicz*

Can a shape with corners roll beneath a board without making it rise and fall? This prototype introduces constant width through a sequence of experiments, a geometric construction, and a bicycle design challenge.

## Learning goals

By the end of the course, learners should be able to:

- explain width as the distance between two parallel boards touching a shape;
- recognize the Reuleaux triangle and tell it apart from other triangles with curved sides;
- explain, using the compass construction, why the Reuleaux triangle has constant width;
- explain why an ordinary bike with its wheels replaced by Reuleaux triangles would not give a level ride.

## About this project

The project presents my approach to mathematical content creation: choosing an accessible question, sequencing experiments around plausible misconceptions, and helping learners connect what they observe to a geometric reason.

**How this was made.** The idea, the mathematical content, and the lesson design are my own: the choice of topic, the sequence of experiments and questions, the misconceptions each activity targets, and the feedback. The interactive implementation (code, animations, and interface) was built with Claude, an AI assistant by Anthropic, working under my direction. I designed every screen, reviewed each version, and made all content and design decisions. AI also suggested edits to the English text.
